Weight in sets

Same weight across all sets (straight sets) — choose a weight you can hit the lower rep range with in every set.

Example: Bench press 4×5–7 at 100 kg — all 4 sets at 100 kg.

Pyramid sets have a flaw — early sets too light, last set too heavy. Only 1–2 effective sets instead of 4.

When to increase weight

  • 1Complete the upper rep range in all sets (e.g. 4×7 with range 5–7) → add 2.5 kg next session.
  • 2After increasing, drop back to lower range (e.g. 4×5) and build back up. This is double progression.
  • 3For isolations and finishers, add 1.25 kg or one rep — slower progression is normal.
  • 4Can't hit the lower range for 2 weeks in a row — reduce by 5–10% and rebuild.

Warm-up

Before the first compound of each session — 2–3 warm-up sets, never to failure:

Set 1: 40% × 10 · Set 2: 60% × 6 · Set 3: 80% × 3

Goal: warm up joints, prime the nervous system, enter working sets fully ready.

Deload — every 6–8 weeks

Deload week: 50–60% of normal weight · 2–3 sets instead of 4 · same rep range but with 2–3 reps in reserve · cardio and abs unchanged.

Supercompensation fully happens during deload. Personal records often follow.
Session length: Push and Pull ~70 min · Legs ~75 min · + 25 min cardio · total ~95–100 min. Use a timer for rest periods.

💪 Muscle Gain (Lean Bulk)
Add +200–250 kcal above your TDEE. Aim for 0.5–1 kg gain per month — more than that means excess fat.
🔥 Fat Loss (Cut)
Subtract 300–400 kcal from your TDEE. Keep protein high to protect muscle. Aim for 0.5–0.75 kg loss per week.
Key rules:
· Protein: always 2.0–2.5 g per kg of bodyweight — this is non-negotiable
· Carbs: your main energy source, adjust based on training intensity
· Fat: minimum 0.8 g/kg for hormonal health, don't go below
· Weigh yourself weekly (same day, same time) — 4-week average matters more than daily fluctuation

📊 Body Measurements
How to measure correctly:
· Waist — at navel level, exhale naturally, don't suck in
· Chest — across nipple line, arms relaxed at sides
· Bicep — flexed, at widest point of dominant arm
· Shoulders — fullest part, arms at sides, tape parallel to floor
· Hips/Glutes — widest part of hips/buttocks
· Thigh — upper thigh, just below the glute fold, dominant leg
Measure in the morning, before eating, same conditions each time.

ℹ️ How to Use This App
💪 Training Tabs
Push, Pull and Legs are your three main training days. Whole Body is a 4th, optional full-body session. Each exercise shows LT (Last Training weight), SB (Season Best) and PB (Personal Best) — tap LT to log a new weight, tap SB or PB to reset them. The ↑ Increase button flags an exercise so you remember to add weight next session; it clears automatically once you log a heavier LT. The round ⏱️ button starts a rest timer for that exercise — it keeps running even if you switch tabs or leave the app.
✏️ Editing Exercises
Swipe left/right on an exercise (or use the ‹ › arrows) to see alternative exercises for that slot. Edit changes an exercise's name, notes, sets, reps, rest time and type — and lets you delete it. ＋ Add Alternative adds a new option to that slot. The Abs exercise at the end of every session is shared: editing it, or logging a weight on it, updates it everywhere at once.
💡 Tips
Tap 💡 Tips on any exercise for a full-screen view with LT/SB/PB, the rest timer, and a place to write your own instructions (bold, underline, bullet lists) plus an optional YouTube link for form reference.
📅 Calendar
Tap any day to log a completed session or plan a future one. Planned days show a dashed outline; once the date arrives you can mark it Done or cancel it. Mark a session as a Deload week when logging it. On the entry list below the calendar, swipe an entry left to delete or right to edit.
📊 Body Stats
Log waist, chest, arms, shoulders, hips, thigh, weight and body fat %. Add up to 3 progress photos (front/side/back) from your camera or photo library — they're automatically compressed so your data stays small. Tap a photo to view it full-screen.
📈 Charts
Line charts of every Body Stats measurement over the last 12 months, plus a pie chart of how many days you've trained versus how many days have passed this year.
🖼️ Dashboard
Your home screen: last completed session, next planned one (tap it to jump straight to that training tab), current weekly consistency streaks, weeks since your last deload, total training days, and two leaderboards showing which exercises haven't had a new SB or PB in the longest time — a nudge to push those lifts again.
🥗 Diet
Enter your age, weight, height, body fat % and training frequency to get a daily calorie and macro target (Katch-McArdle formula), plus separate targets for a lean bulk and a cut.
⚙️ Settings
Export your data (all of it, or just exercises, calendar, or body stats) as a JSON file you can back up or move to another device. Import restores from a backup. You can also clear specific data — like just your SB records — without wiping everything.
🔔 Notifications & Offline
Once added to your Home Screen, the app works offline and keeps the screen awake during a session. Rest timer alerts can arrive as push notifications even when the app isn't open, powered by a small free relay service.
🙌 About This App
This app was designed and built by Marcin Zająkała from Poland, working together with Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant — describing what to build and refining it turn by turn, with Claude writing all the actual code. Want to get in touch? Reach out on LinkedIn.
It's open source — a single self-contained HTML file with no paid backend, hosted for free. It was built entirely with free tools:
  • GitHub Pages — free static hosting for the app itself
  • Documents by Readdle — used to preview and test changes on iPhone before publishing
  • ntfy.sh — a free, open-source push notification service
  • Render.com — free-tier server that schedules and relays rest-timer notifications
  • Standard, built-in web technology — HTML, CSS and JavaScript, Service Workers for offline use, and SVG for the charts — no paid frameworks or libraries
During the refinement phase — polishing features, fixing layout issues and adding new sections like this one — a paid Claude subscription was used, powered by Anthropic's Sonnet and Opus models.
